## Faster isn't the same as better

Make the oven faster and the queue just moves to the person plating. When one task gets quicker, the pressure lands on the next person along. So before speeding anything up, I ask where the pressure would go next. (From how I think about systems: https://onequestionlater.com/work/gp-systems)

## Ask why it exists before asking how to speed it up

If a report takes a week, the obvious fix is a faster report. The better question is who needs to see what, and when, and whether they'd decide anything differently if they could. Changing who sees what usually does more than changing a number. (https://onequestionlater.com/work/gp-systems)

## Map the real work, then take things out

I map how the work actually happens, not how the process document says it does. Then I look for the gaps, the patterns and the loops, and I start by taking things out. The part of the Digital Feasibility Study I'm proudest of didn't start with making anything. (https://onequestionlater.com/work/digital-feasibility-study)

## Not everything needs AI

Some of what I've deployed uses AI and some doesn't. The test is the same either way: does this give someone their time back? (https://onequestionlater.com/work/gp-systems)

## The first limit is rarely the real one

The first version of the proposal system brought three weeks down to four days. So the three weeks were never the real limit. Rebuilt end to end with multi-agent workflows, it now takes about seven minutes. (https://onequestionlater.com/work/proposal-system)

## Someone has to own it afterwards

I train the people around me to own these systems, so a one-off fix turns into how the organisation works. Manual workload dropped by about 35%, and the bigger change is that it stuck. (https://onequestionlater.com/work/gp-systems)

## Use AI, or wield it

One set of people use AI like an advanced Google search. The other set wield it. Training starts with a leader's own organisation and live decisions, not a curriculum. (https://onequestionlater.com/work/catalyst)

## Private data doesn't have to stop you

Teams pasting confidential documents into AI face two bad choices: retype everything to strip out names, or paste it in and hope. Giving every private detail a stand-in, then putting the real details back, removes the choice. (https://onequestionlater.com/work/penname)

## Structure before paint

When a website is failing the people it's there to serve, a new coat of paint won't fix it. I rebuild from the structure up: information architecture first, then performance. (https://onequestionlater.com/work/gp-website)
