## How I start a conversation about a problem

I don't start with tools. I start with the person and the work: what they do all day, what keeps coming back, and what they'd do with the time if they got it back. If you got two hours back every day, where would you spend them? The answer tells me more than any requirements document.

## The questions I ask, in order

First, what's the problem, in your words? Then, what would good look like, and how would you know it worked? Then, how does it actually happen today, step by step, and who touches it? Then, what does it cost you now, in hours, money or missed chances? Only after that: the constraints. Rough budget, when it needs to work by, the tools you already use, and who decides. I ask one at a time. A brief built from good answers beats a long form filled in quickly.

## How I reframe a problem

Most problems arrive as a request for something faster. I flip it: earlier we bought software and bent ourselves to fit it, now we can build around how you already work. So I ask why the task exists, who needs to see what and when, and where the pressure would move if one step got quicker. Often the better fix is changing who sees what, not making a number smaller.

## What I need before I can scope anything

Five things: the problem in your words, what success looks like, how it works today, one real example (a document, an email, a call), and who will own it once it's built. With those I can give you a first read and the open questions for a call. Without them, anything I scoped would be a guess.

## Talking to people who aren't technical

You don't need the vocabulary. Describe your day and what frustrates you, and I'll do the translating. If I use a word you don't know, that's my mistake, not yours. Most of the people I build with run charities, restaurants, consultancies and boards, not engineering teams.

## What a first read looks like

One reframe of the problem, two or three first steps, a similar thing I've built and what I learned from it, and the one question that would sharpen the picture. It's a starting point to refine together, never a quote or a promise.
