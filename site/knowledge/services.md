## The shapes my work usually takes

Most of what I do falls into a few shapes. A system built around one painful process, like proposals, invoices, or bookings over the phone. An assistant that answers from an organisation's own documents. One-to-one sessions where I set up a leader's own AI stack around how they already work. Training for leadership teams. And websites, rebuilt from the structure up. Through Philanthropel, I build these with non-profits, foundations and philanthropists, on their own data and theirs to run afterwards.

## How a project usually runs

It starts with a conversation and one real example of the work. Then I map how the work actually happens, take things out, and build the smallest version that gives someone time back. People use it with real work early, and I adjust from what they tell me. It ends with training the people who'll own it, so it becomes how they work rather than something that depends on me.

## Setting up your own AI stack

Some of my work is one-to-one: a founder, a board member or a leader who wants AI working across their own email, calendar, files and notes. We pick one base for email and files, connect the tools to it, set up a folder per workstream so context doesn't blur, and build a morning briefing they actually read. These usually run over a few sessions, each one ending with a short list of what to set up before the next.

## What I don't take on

I don't sell software licences or one-size-fits-all chatbots, and I don't build things no one will own afterwards. I'm not the right person for large enterprise IT migrations. I'd rather tell you that early than take the project.
