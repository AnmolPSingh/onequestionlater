## Ideas I'm exploring for the charity sector

Two ideas I keep coming back to, both for charities and the people who fund them. They're ideas and early shapes, not products you can sign up for yet. If one sounds like your problem, that's exactly the conversation I want to have.

## A grant fit check, before the writing

Grant seeking is a gamble with no gate at the front. Teams put forty hours or more into a proposal that was never going to land, and when it loses, all that time feels wasted. Fundraisers told me this before I went looking for it. AI that writes grants is everywhere now, so that isn't the gap. The gap is the question before it: should we even bother? The shape is simple. You paste in the grant and your mission, criteria and track record. It tells you pursue or skip, with reasons. For the ones worth pursuing, it shows where your application is thin and how to line it up better. It only needs public grant text and your own profile, never a donor's personal data.

## Tracking donor movement, not just money

A fundraiser with fifteen years in the sector told me something that stuck: charities track the money, but they don't track donor movement. A second one said almost the same thing the next day, without being asked. Teams are so busy logging gifts that the relationship goes untended, and only about one in five first-year donors gives again. That's a relationship problem, not a money problem. So the idea is to track the relationship itself: the last meaningful contact, whether someone is warming up or drifting, where they are in their journey, and to flag the donors who are slipping before they're gone.

## Thanking donors at scale without losing the warmth

A campaign funded by twenty donors gets handwritten thank-yous. The same campaign funded by twenty thousand gets a template, if that. The idea: when impact data comes in, match each donor to what their gift actually did, draft a personal thank-you, and have a person approve it before it goes out by email, text or, for donors who opt in, a warm voice call. Two rules hold throughout. Donors always know when AI is involved, and it's thanks, never a sales pitch. And it supports the fundraiser's relationship rather than faking or replacing it. I'd start by running it by hand with one charity and one campaign, so we prove it moves retention before building anything bigger.
