Talking Philanthropy held its first Summit in Geneva on 19 November 2026. The first guest app was a single page. Check-ins went into a record nobody could read on the day, and the door ran on iPads.

I rebuilt it as one app for the whole day. Guests check in with their name and organisation, which is matched against the confirmed in-person list, so nobody waits for an email. Each guest carries a QR pass on their phone. At the door, a volunteer scans it, and the name runs ahead to the desk in the order people were scanned. The lanyard is ready before the guest reaches it. Tap Given, and the name clears on every team phone.

Guests, speakers and the team each get their own side of the same app.

## Everything it does

### For guests

- **Check in** with your name and organisation, matched against the confirmed in-person list, with no email code to wait for. Anyone who used the old app is welcomed back and signed straight in.
- **Pick your interests** from ten topics, so the people who share them can find you.
- **Carry your pass.** A personal QR pass sits on Home. Your phone tells you when you're checked in, and again when your lanyard is handed over.
- **Home follows the day.** Before, it counts down, with a get-ready checklist built from what you've done and people you should meet. On the morning, it shows the full-size pass and the route. During the sessions, it shows what's live and next, updates, and who's here who shares your interests. Afterwards, it says thank you, shows the people you met, and asks for reflections.
- **Programme.** Every session and speaker. Star a session to build your own schedule.
- **People.** Filter by interest, connect, message in real time, and introduce two people in your network to each other.
- **Questions for the panels.** Send up to two to the stage. People watching the livestream can send theirs without the app.
- **Book time with the team.** Meet in Geneva or call afterwards, in 15, 20 or 30 minute slots. The booking lands in that person's Outlook calendar, with a Teams link for calls. A slot can never be booked twice, and either side can cancel.
- **Places and the event guide.** The venue map, getting there, where to stay, accessibility, and the venue Wi-Fi on the day.
- **Resources.** Speaker slides and reports, open inside the app.
- **Notifications.** A bell in the app, alerts on the lock screen, and an unread count on the icon.
- **Reflections.** A short questionnaire once the day ends.
- **Your profile,** with a photo from the camera or your library.

### For speakers

- **A portal of their own.** Speakers sign in with a personal PIN and see their own sessions, their public profile, their contact on the team, and notes for before and on the day.
- **Kept apart from networking.** Speakers aren't part of guest networking, and the database enforces it, not just the screens.

### For the team and volunteers

- **Team portal.** Everyone signs in with their own PIN. Scan a pass, or check someone in by name, including guests without the app. A second scan never counts twice.
- **Lanyard desk.** Names arrive in the order people were scanned. Tap Given, and the name clears on every team phone.
- **My availability.** The booking team opens their slots, and any time they're busy in Outlook is skipped.
- **Guest questions,** grouped by session, ready to pick the ones that go to the moderator.
- **Admin.** Post updates to Home, add a late guest and send their arrival email, manage volunteers, the booking team and PINs, download the reflections, and read the arrivals report: confirmed, arrived, the show-up rate, who hasn't come yet, and a spreadsheet.

## How it fits together

It's one web app on every phone, with three portals inside it. It installs to the home screen, updates itself, and lives at the same website link as before. Three small services do the work that touches the outside: check-in matches guests against the in-person list in Mailchimp, then records and tags each arrival; booking reads and writes the team's Outlook calendars; and notifications reach the bell and the lock screen. Everything else sits in one database whose access rules follow each role, so speakers can't see guests, and admins can't read private messages. A PIN locks after too many wrong guesses.

## What I learned

An event is a run of small waits. Most of the work was moving each one earlier: the check against the guest list before the door, the lanyard before the desk.
