# Design and narrative brief

## What the current site assessment established

The site is One Question Later, Anmol Pratap Singh’s personal portfolio. The current experience is built in Astro with a particle portrait and a scroll sequence that includes an approach statement, an FM-radio scene, a career session, global reach, project slides, and a desert closing. The source and saved review images informed the initial assessment. The particle portrait, restrained dark atmosphere, and distinctive transitions give the experience a recognisable identity. A saved mobile performance report scored 79 against the project’s target of 90. A fresh check is still needed for the current site.

Anmol likes the first two scrolls and dislikes the diagram on the second. The extended radio-noise and tuning sequence delays both the journey and the work. The work arrived too late, and the full-screen project slides made browsing slower. The timeline matters to Anmol as an experience in its own right and as evidence of how he developed his approach.

## How the arc evolved

The early suggestion put the journey before the projects, with the radio compressed or removed. Anmol wanted visitors to encounter the work sooner and asked for a deeper rethink. The agreed direction preserves the opening, brings selected work forward, and lets the journey explain the capabilities visitors have already seen in action.

| Order | Section | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meet Anmol | Keep the particle portrait and the breathing room of the first screen. Make Work directly reachable. |
| 2 | Approach | Keep the shape and pace of the second screen. Replace the diagram with a clear statement about how Anmol works, then lead directly into an actual project. |
| 3 | Selected Work | Show the four new case previews together and let visitors choose which to open. Give each its own page. Place the rest of the work after the featured cases. |
| 4 | Journey | Keep the immersive timeline. Offer a compact overview with selectable moments and connect earlier experiences to current work where the relationship is supported. |
| 5 | The next question | A short closing moment and direct access to contact. The desert may remain as a brief close if it serves the experience. |

Remove the separate FM-radio chapter from the main sequence. Put global reach alongside relevant work instead of making it another stop. Aim to show the first project immediately after the two opening screens. Make motion connect ideas and explain what is happening while keeping projects and timeline moments directly reachable. Give desktop and mobile visitors equivalent access and retain the existing reduced-motion and readable-content requirements.

## Positioning

Anmol wants the portfolio to show a systems thinker and builder who can work across environments. He described helping businesses ranging from restaurants and small businesses to large enterprises, and training senior leaders on AI adoption and digital transformation. Private equity, finance, and banking came up as examples of audiences he wants to reach. Use verified engagements for claims about past work.

His approach pairs a client’s domain knowledge with design thinking, systems thinking, and practical building. He listens to the people doing the work, traces how information, decisions, and tools connect, and identifies where the original problem framing or workflow can change. An idea developed in one setting can help solve a problem in another. He tests whether it fits the new constraints and carries the work through the technical details, the user experience, and iteration. Adoption and training can be part of that work.

The portfolio should make this reasoning visible. Show the actual problem, the insight that changed the approach, Anmol’s contribution, and the evidence of what changed. His mission-driven work remains part of the story, while the approach applies more broadly.

At ExpoBazaar, Anmol was the sole UX designer. His work extended into understanding backend logic, collaborating with the technical team, testing, and repeated refinement. Describe the scope accurately using his actual role. It is a journey example of how he learned to connect what people experience with how the underlying system works. His AI experiments began alongside UX. The timeline should show these experiences accumulating and reconnecting, with room for detours and earlier interests to recur.

## Accepted philosophy extension and approach draft

### One Question Later

I follow questions. Occasionally they turn into careers.

Each question leaves me with something I carry forward. Music gave me a feel for experimentation and rhythm. Storytelling taught me to listen and make connections. Design taught me to understand people. Working with technology taught me how those experiences connect to the systems underneath.

Over time, that changes what I notice.

An idea from one environment can become useful in another. A familiar problem can look different when approached through a different discipline. Something I learned years ago can help me ask a better question today.

One Question Later is also about those connections, how what I have learned changes what I can see and what I can help make possible.

### Bring another way of seeing, then make it work

I start with the people doing the work. They bring knowledge of their field, its constraints, and the details that matter. I bring experience connecting people, information, decisions, and technology.

Together, we look beyond the requested feature to understand what needs to change. Sometimes the opportunity is a better tool. Sometimes it is a different flow of information, a decision made earlier, or a process that can be reconsidered altogether.

I draw on patterns from other environments and test whether they belong here. Design thinking helps me understand the human need. Systems thinking helps me see how a change affects the whole. Building and testing turn that possibility into something people can use.

My involvement continues through the details, from how the logic works and how the experience feels to where it breaks and what needs another iteration. Helping people adopt it is part of making it work.

## Typography and visual language

Anmol felt the combination of Newsreader, Geist Sans, and Geist Mono did not reflect his personality. The two qualities he wanted were energy and directness. Archivo, Space Grotesk, and Manrope were rendered using the same wording, palette, sizes, and weights. He chose **Space Grotesk throughout the website**, including the home, project, journey, and philosophy views.

Use one main typographic voice, with bold for key statements and project titles, regular for reading, and medium for navigation, dates, and other supporting details. Use scale, weight, spacing, and rhythm to give the statements energy. The saved specimen shows weights 400, 500, and 700. The full design system, including a final type scale and component styling, is still open.

## How to showcase the projects

The initial shortlist was the proposal system, Penname, and ExpoBazaar. Anmol later introduced five AI projects and asked for them to lead Selected Work. He completed four stories and put the evaluation platform on hold. The order is document assistant, voice receptionist, invoice processing, and research assistant. Give each a separate entry. The visitor chooses the project, and each detail page can open with a short outcome, a visual, the fuller case, and what Anmol learned.

The first diagrams were plain flow sketches. Anmol found the flows understandable and wanted a more considered visual treatment. He confirmed [Anatomy](https://github.com/wheresryan22/anatomy) as the isometric reference and explicitly requested figures made with it for all four. He also shared [Diagram Design](https://github.com/cathrynlavery/diagram-design) as an optional architecture reference and delegated the choice of how to use it.

| Case | Anatomy figure | What the route shows |
|---|---|---|
| Document assistant | Filing cabinet, incoming trays, and retrieval tray | Contributions from several people join a shared client record, where earlier work can be found. |
| Voice receptionist | Telephone, switchboard, and two receiving desks | A table booking reaches the correct front desk. A catering request reaches the owner with the details captured. |
| Invoice processing | Invoice tray, scanner, review desk, and outgoing tray | The assistant processes documents and prepares a draft. The admin’s approval comes before sending. |
| Research assistant | Source tray, evidence cabinet, research desk, and two outputs | Search adds sources to the collection. The answering step cites evidence or reports what it cannot support. |

Match the existing particle language. Use a dark charcoal canvas, visible grey surfaces and pale edges, restrained amber points of light that follow a route, and Space Grotesk. The first image export lost its shading and produced nearly black silhouettes on a brown background. The corrected render uses explicit export colours and brighter grey geometry. Treat the corrected board as the visual reference. In the HTML, select a workflow step to highlight the route, or open the architecture summary for a simpler view.

Use generic records and illustrative geometry throughout. The cases have confidentiality constraints on demos and repositories. The diagrams explain publicly shareable workflows and should be checked against the case notes before publication.
