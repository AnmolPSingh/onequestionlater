## 01 Music

**H:** Be curious enough to make a sound.

Before design, there was music. I studied audio engineering and digital music production, as hooked on the software as I was on the sound.

Making a track works like this. You have a thought. You make sounds that resemble it. You arrange them until someone else feels something. I didn't know it then, but that was my first design process.

I still make electronic music, and I still release it.

**Pull:** Same craft, different material. It took me ten years to notice.

## 02 Learn the tool

**H:** Be curious enough to learn the tool.

My first tracks on SoundCloud needed artwork. I had MS Paint and a lot of confidence. A friend told me about Photoshop, so I learned it from YouTube to make covers for my own music.

Then I started playing gigs, and gigs needed posters. My first ones were bad. I got better by rebuilding posters I loved, piece by piece, until I understood why they worked.

**Pull:** I've never learned a tool to put it on a CV. I learned each one because there was something I wanted to make.

**Caption:** Delhi Raasta, 2014. Gigs needed posters.

**Tag:** Tool unlocked: Photoshop

## 03 Ask the question nobody asks

**H:** Be curious enough to ask the question nobody asks.

Then came the respectable degree, because middle-class Indian parents. Journalism and mass communication at Amity.

Radio first. A summer at All India Radio turned into on-call work, and taught me that a listener can't see you, so every word has to earn its place.

Then film. I learned Premiere from YouTube and directed a public service short that won first prize at college. Then newsrooms, at Doordarshan and BBC India.

Journalism taught me the thing design school later confirmed. Don't start with what you want to say. Start with what you need to understand.

**Pull:** The brief is evidence. It isn't always the problem.

**Captions:**
- All India Radio. Filming wasn't allowed, so here's the desk.
- On a short film with Doordarshan and BBC India.

**Tag:** Tool unlocked: Premiere Pro

## 04 Say yes before you know how

**H:** Be curious enough to say yes before you know how.

My first job was junior graphic designer. The pay was bad enough that freelancing looked safer, so I messaged everyone I'd ever worked with and offered a friendly rate. It turns out a friendly rate is mostly friendly to them.

Audio one month, video the next, scripts after that. The range was the product.

Then a client said: you're good at Photoshop, can you design the screens for my app?

I had no background in UX.

[Scroll into empty space, then:]

**H2:** So naturally, I said yes.

Then I learned UX. I don't see it as changing disciplines. It was the same job in a different mode.

**Tag:** Tool unlocked: UX

## 05 The only designer in the room

**H:** Be curious enough to be the only designer in the room.

That app took me to a startup, ExpoBazaar, where I was the only designer for a year and a half. Merchandising, development, the VP of Tech, project management and marketing. Five departments, and none of them spoke design.

So I learned to speak theirs. The dashboards I designed raised supplier engagement by 25%, and that number won the argument faster than any design rationale ever did.

**Pull:** Speak their language first. They'll learn yours later.

**Captions:**
- Delhi Fair. Onboarding artisans from across India to sell to the world.
- Designing for foreign markets.
- Not forgetting to have fun, here and there.

## 07 Remove things

[Back to dark]

**H:** Be curious enough to remove things.

Today I'm a design technologist at Global Philanthropic, a philanthropy consultancy working across the UK, Europe and Asia. I joined as the designer. Then I kept running into problems that design alone couldn't solve, so I started experimenting with AI and automation, and building systems around it.

The work I'm proudest of didn't start with making anything. It started with understanding how the work actually happened, finding the gaps and the patterns, and taking things out. Only then did I build something better, and it became a product clients pay for.

**Pull:** Designers don't always have to arrive with an answer. Sometimes the job is to take something away.

Then:

One set of people use AI like an advanced Google search. The other set wield it. Most of what I do now is help people move from the first group to the second.

## The pattern

Music. Radio. Film. Graphic design. UX. XR. AI.

It looks like seven careers. It's one pattern. I wanted to make something, I didn't know the tool, so I learned it, and the tool became the next thing.

**H:** The only constant was curiosity.

Being curious was never the hard part. Being curious enough to start, badly, before I knew how: that was the whole trick.
